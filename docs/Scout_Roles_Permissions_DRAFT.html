Scout — Practice Roles & Permissions

Draft for approval · Vet At Your Door · practice-level roles only (platform / multi-tenant roles excluded)

What this is. A proposed default role set for a Scout practice, and who can do what across the whole product. It is modeled on the conventional veterinary PIMS baseline you supplied, then rewritten against Scout's actual screens and actions — mobile-practice specifics (routing, zones, room loader, forward booking, autoship, post-visit membership re-rating) have no equivalent in a conventional PIMS, so those rows are new.

What I need from you. Approve or edit the role list in Section 1, the retirements in Section 2, the matrix in Section 4, and the open decisions in Section 7. Nothing is implemented yet.

Why these three are new

  • Veterinarian (DVM) — the significant gap. Scout's role catalog today has no provider role at all. Record signing, Rx approval, and provider-scoped production reporting have no role to attach to. This is the one addition I would not skip.
  • Billing & AR — refunds, voids, deposits, and reconciliation are currently only reachable by making someone a full admin. A narrow role lets a bookkeeper work without clinical or configuration access. Optional — see Decision 2 if you would rather fold this into Lead Client Liaison.
  • Observer — there is no way today to give someone look-only access. Useful for onboarding and for your accountant. Optional — see Decision 5.

5. Conventions & guardrails behind the matrix

  1. Only a DVM signs. Sign & lock, addendum after lock, Rx approval, and refill authorization are DVM-only, with no admin override. An administrator who is also licensed holds the DVM role in addition to Practice Admin. This is the one place where "admin can do everything" is deliberately false.
  2. Price changes and discounts are capped, not blocked. Providers, technicians, and liaisons can discount within a practice cap with a mandatory reason code; anything larger routes to Practice Manager. The discount/void/refund audit report is the real control — a hard block just pushes people into giving away free line items instead.
  3. Refunds stay open to client-facing roles. The usual industry guardrail (same-day void only, or a dollar ceiling) does not survive your post-visit membership flow, where a client enrolls after the appointment and the visit is re-rated retroactively. Refunds therefore require a reason code and show on the audit report rather than being restricted by role.
  4. Removing a line item is an audit event. Technicians and providers can remove their own entries only. Every removal appears on the void/discount report, because a silent removal is a missed charge.
  5. Lead versus non-lead. The difference is consistently: team task reassignment, record merges, same-day financial reversals, and team reporting. Everything clinical or client-facing is identical.
  6. "Own vehicle" for a mobile practice. Providers and technicians adjust and move stock on the van they are assigned to; branch and depot stock stays with Inventory Manager. Inventory Manager and Practice Manager are branch-scoped roles, so a person can hold them for one location only.
  7. Inventory Manager owns cost, not sell price. They set cost, receive, count, and reorder; manual sell-price overrides and bulk price changes stay with Practice Manager and above. This is the common split and it is what your sample assumed.
  8. Observer is enforced by hiding controls, not by disabling them. An Observer should never see a Save button they cannot press.
  9. Roles stack. Someone who is both Lead Technician and Inventory Manager gets the union of both. No role subtracts from another.
  10. Platform access is never assignable from practice settings. Feature flags, domains, secrets, and the practice list stay with Vet At Your Door.

7. Decisions I need from you

  1. Discount cap. What percentage can a DVM, technician, or liaison apply before it needs Practice Manager approval? The common setting is 10–15%.
  2. Billing & AR — keep it or fold it in? If your liaisons already handle collections, delete the role and give Lead Client Liaison the refund, deposit, and reconciliation rows instead.
  3. Can a Practice Manager create staff logins? The matrix says yes for non-admin logins. Say the word if that should be Practice Admin only.
  4. Voiding a deposit. Currently Practice Admin and Practice Manager, with Billing & AR needing approval. Should Billing & AR be able to do it outright?
  5. Observer — needed? Worth having for onboarding and for an outside bookkeeper, but it is a real role to build and test.
  6. Relief / locum DVMs. Should they be a separate role (same clinical rights, no production reports, no access after their last shift), or just the DVM role deactivated when they leave?
  7. Branch scoping. Inventory Manager and Practice Manager are branch-specific today. Should Lead Technician or Lead Client Liaison also be scoped per location?
  8. Who approves a lab result into the record? The matrix says DVM only, with Lead Technician able to flag. Confirm that matches how your team actually works.
  9. Reason codes and the audit report. Build them with the roles, or ship roles first and add the audit layer in a second phase?
  10. Anything a specific person needs that the role they will hold does not allow. Easier to catch now than after rollout.
